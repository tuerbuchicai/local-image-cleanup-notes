# Repair verification checklist

A small local repair either holds up or it does not. These are the checks that decide it, before and after.

## Before you paint

1. **What is behind the mark.** Plain sky, paper, a wall, or a gently changing colour gives a local repair something believable to continue. A face, lettering, or a repeating pattern does not.
2. **The edge that matters.** Look at the boundary the repair will touch. A straight edge, a hard shadow line, or a fine texture is where a patch shows first.
3. **The scale.** Measure the mark against the frame. A small label is a local repair. A banner across the image is not, and no amount of retrying changes that.

## After you repair

1. **Look at 100%.** Judge the result at full size, not in a fitted preview where the damage is invisible.
2. **Look at fit-to-screen.** Judge it again the way the final image will be seen.
3. **Compare against the untouched neighbours.** Put the repaired area next to pixels the tool never touched. If the texture, grain, or colour direction changes, the repair is visible even when the mark is gone.

## When to stop

If a face, an edge, lettering, or a repeated pattern changes, clear the mark and retry with a tighter selection. If it still changes, the area is larger or more detailed than this workflow fits, and the honest answer is that this method is the wrong tool for that image.
